Food & Diet

A balanced diet is key to a long, healthy life for your guinea pig. Their diet should be made up of unlimited timothy hay (80%), fresh leafy greens & veggies (15%), and a small amount of high-quality pellets (5%). Guinea pigs cannot produce their own Vitamin C, so daily fresh vegetables are essential! Learn what's safe, what to avoid, and how to build a diet your piggies will love.

Unlimited timothy hay Daily fresh veggies Vitamin C essential No iceberg lettuce

Daily Care

Guinea pigs thrive on routine and consistency! Daily care involves freshening their hay, providing fresh water, cleaning their food bowl, and spending quality time with them. Weekly tasks include a deeper cage clean and nail check. Every few weeks you'll want to trim nails and check teeth. Long-haired breeds need regular brushing too. Use the videos below to master every aspect of guinea pig grooming and care!

Health & Vet Care

Guinea pigs are prey animals, which means they instinctively hide signs of illness. It's crucial to do daily health checks and know what's normal for your pet. Common health issues include respiratory infections, dental problems, skin conditions, and bladder stones. Always seek an exotic vet experienced with guinea pigs — not all vets are trained for small animals. Check out the videos below to learn how to spot illness early and what to expect at the vet!

Bonding & Handling

Guinea pigs are incredibly social creatures — in the wild, they live in herds! Keeping at least two guinea pigs together is strongly recommended for their emotional well-being. Bonding two guinea pigs takes patience and a proper introduction process. When it comes to handling, start slow, always support their bottom, and let them come to you. With time and consistency, guinea pigs can become very affectionate and confident companions!

Keep in pairs or groups Slow introductions Support their bottom Build trust daily
